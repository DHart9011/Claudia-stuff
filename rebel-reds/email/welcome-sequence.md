**Preview Text:** Let's talk about safety. Directly and honestly.

Hey,

I'm going to skip past the pleasantries and get to the question I hear from almost everyone before they book their first flight:

*Is it safe?*

Fair question. Here's a straight answer.

The biggest thing to understand about a gyroplane is how the rotor works. Unlike a helicopter, the rotor on a gyroplane is not powered by the engine. It spins freely — it autorotates — because air flows up through it as the aircraft moves forward. That's just physics. The rotor keeps spinning whether the engine is running or not.

What that means practically: if the engine stops, you don't fall out of the sky. The rotor is already spinning. You glide down at a controlled, predictable rate and land in a short distance. A gyroplane in a power-off glide handles very similarly to a fully powered one — just a little steeper descent.

You cannot stall a gyroplane the way you can stall a fixed-wing aircraft. There's no angle-of-attack issue with a freely spinning rotor. You can slow it down to almost walking speed and it stays flying.

Now — none of this means it's impossible to fly unsafely. Pilot error is always a factor in any aircraft. Proper training matters. That's why I take instruction seriously. Every student I train learns the aircraft correctly before they fly solo, and I don't rush that process.

Gyroplanes have a strong safety record compared to other light aircraft when flown by properly trained pilots. If you want to dig into the specifics on that, call me and I'll talk through the numbers with you.

The bottom line: the physics of a gyroplane are on your side in ways that a lot of aircraft are not. That's not marketing — that's how autorotation works.
